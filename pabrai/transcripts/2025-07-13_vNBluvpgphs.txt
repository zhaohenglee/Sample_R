Mohnish: Yes. Let us take the mental model of cloning. What we are taught is that if 
you want to start a business, you need to come up with something new, 
something that has not been done before. But the reality is that the world 
will easily accept three to five of the same thing. It is an advantage to look 
at something that already exists and say, “Can another one of those exist? 
Can I take what is there and tweak it a little bit?” There is something peculiar 
in the human psyche, maybe going back into our history and our ancestral 
evolution, where humans look down upon cloning. But if you look at it, two 
of the greatest cloners in human history were Bill Gates and Sam Walton. 
We think of Bill Gates as an innovator, and we think Sam Walton created 
Walmart, which was also new, but as a matter of fact, they are both “Me 
Too” models. Microsoft would not have existed without being a great cloner. 
When we look at Microsoft Word, it came from WordPerfect. We look at 
Excel, which came from Lotus. We look at Bing, which came from Google. 
Everything that Microsoft has done well has come from copying someone 
on the outside. When we look at Sam Walton, from the Walton family, if you 
pull them all together, it is the richest family in the world. It is richer than 
Elon and everyone. Sam Walton, by his own admission, would tell you that 
he has no original ideas. Walmart cloned Sears and Kmart.
