One of the things I had discussed with Charlie is whether these

Charlie said that Warren Buffett had studied a large number of Coke

players decided that they wanted to increase market share. They 
became more aggressive with promotions and pricing. Of course, 
the second player has to respond to that. Even though there is brand 
loyalty, if I go to pick up a 12-pack of Coke and the 12-pack of Pepsi 
is 30% cheaper, I would switch. There is brand loyalty, but it is not 
extreme brand loyalty. In these markets where these players were 
trying to take more market share from the other player, nobody 
made any money because it was a race to the bottom. The other 
person also reacted, and then pricing went down and got more 
aggressive. You end up with markets where normally having a Coke

It is the way it is. The investing business is an extremely forgiving

more than acceptable if you are following some rules.  
 
Let me give you an example. In the late '60s and early 1970s in the 
U.S., there was a concept known as the Nifty 50. The Nifty 50 was an 
approach to investing where the idea was that you bought equal 
amounts invested in the 50 strongest, most blue-chip companies in 
the U.S. You would take a portfolio and put 2% of the portfolio into 
American Express, 2% in Coca-Cola, Pepsi, GE, Kodak, Boeing, 
McDonald's, Polaroid, and so on. You would not care about the 
valuations, not care about anything; hold these great businesses, and 
the end result would be good. What ended up happening is that in 
1973 and ‘74, these companies got bid up to spectacular levels, like 
50, 60 times trailing earnings. They got expensive. In '73 and '74, 
there was a stock market crash in slow motion where over two years 
the U.S. stock market went down more than 50%, and the Nifty 50 
went down a lot more than that. It got taken out back and shot. The 
Nifty 50 was probably down like 60, 70%, and by 1975 no one had any 
interest in the Nifty 50. Everyone had sold the Nifty 50 position they 
had at steep losses.  
 
There is some controversy over whether Walmart was part of the 
Nifty 50 or not. But to help me make my point, let us make the

The important thing with investing is what Charlie Munger said is, in
