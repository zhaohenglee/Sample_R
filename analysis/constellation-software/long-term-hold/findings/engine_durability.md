# Engine durability: the six hold tests

The private groups publish no financials, so three of these tests rest on counts and disclosure, not margins.

## 1. Reinvestment runway

What the case needs. At the first report's entry of USD 2,262.86 [hold:hold_grid_10yr.entry_usd_2262_86_first_report:entry_price_usd], ten years of 8 percent FCFA2S per share growth at a 25 times exit returns 8.74 percent a year [hold:hold_grid_10yr.entry_usd_2262_86_first_report:cagr 0.08 exit 25x], and 10 percent growth at 20 times returns 8.31 percent [hold:hold_grid_10yr.entry_usd_2262_86_first_report:cagr 0.10 exit 20x]. At the FX corrected entry of USD 2,080.29 both rise about a point [hold:hold_grid_10yr.entry_usd_2080_29_at_usdcad_1_3594:cagr 0.08 exit 25x] [hold:hold_grid_10yr.entry_usd_2080_29_at_usdcad_1_3594:cagr 0.10 exit 20x]. So the decade needs 8 to 10 percent per share growth every year at a 20 to 25 times exit.

What produces it. The identity is g equals reinvestment rate times return on deployed capital [hold:reinvestment_grid:formula]: 1.00 times FCFA2S at a 10 percent return gives 10 percent growth, at 8 percent it gives 8 percent [hold:reinvestment_grid:reinv 1.00 roic 0.10] [hold:reinvestment_grid:reinv 1.00 roic 0.08]. The required return is far below the stated 25 percent IRR hurdle [ledger:unmapped:Hurdle rate IRR target:2024-2026]. The record: 1.88, 0.63, 1.22 and 0.94 times FCFA2S across FY2022 to FY2025 [metrics:acquisitions_to_fcfa2s:FY2022] [metrics:acquisitions_to_fcfa2s:FY2023] [metrics:acquisitions_to_fcfa2s:FY2024] [metrics:acquisitions_to_fcfa2s:FY2025], a reinvestment rate falling 154.5 to 114.3 to 89.9 percent across 2023 to 2025 [ledger:unmapped:Reinvestment rate:2023, 2024, 2025], and averages of 0.73 times for 2015 to 2020 and 0.85 for 2020 to 2025 on two years [hold:ten_year_history.2015_2020.average_acquisitions_to_fcfa2s] [hold:ten_year_history.2020_2025.average_acquisitions_to_fcfa2s].

The 2026 pace. H1 2026 cash consideration of 1,429 [ledger:acquisitions_cash_spent:H1 2026] against LTM FCFA2S of 2,030 [ledger:fcfa2s:LTM to Q2 2026] is 0.70 of a year's cash flow in six months, computed here. Deployment is not the constraint. Return is: ROIC fell from 14.8 percent in 2025 to 10.97 percent at June 2026 on differing third party definitions [ledger:unmapped:ROIC:2025, June 2026, TTM 2026]. At full deployment and 11 percent the grid just clears the band, no margin above.

Where the runway sits. In 2025 Volaris closed 30 deals [holdings:volaris:acquisitions_per_year:2025], Harris 19 [holdings:harris:acquisitions_per_year:2025], Lumine 15 [stage1:lumine:https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/lumine-group-posts] and Jonas 10 [stage1:jonas:https://jonassoftware.com/2025-year-in-review], the last two stage1 only and not carried into holdings.json; that is 74 deals, computed here, with four groups unreported. 2024 ranked the same [holdings:volaris:acquisitions_per_year:2024] [holdings:perseus:acquisitions_per_year:2024]. Perseus deploys in size instead, 700 million for Optimal Blue and Empower [holdings:perseus:largest_deal_price]; Vela has closed 20 deals in total [holdings:vela:acquisitions_per_year:since_inception]. The runway is two groups wide, one deep.

Verdict: Holds with conditions. Evidence weight: moderate.

## 2. Organic floor

Reported parent organic growth was negative 1, 5, 2 and 4 percent across FY2022 to FY2025 [ledger:organic_growth_pct:FY2022] [ledger:organic_growth_pct:FY2023] [ledger:organic_growth_pct:FY2024] [ledger:organic_growth_pct:FY2025]. FX adjusted it was 3 percent in FY2025, then 2 in Q1 2026 and 1 in Q2 and H1 2026, against 4 percent in Q2 2025 [ledger:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:FY2025] [ledger:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:Q1 2026] [ledger:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:Q2 2026] [ledger:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:H1 2026] [ledger:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:Q2 2025].

The longer record is worse. Average organic growth was negative 0.8 percent for 2015 to 2020 and 0.5 percent across the longest window; the 2.0 percent for 2020 to 2025 rests on two years [hold:ten_year_history.2015_2020.average_organic_growth_pct] [hold:ten_year_history.longest_available.average_organic_growth_pct] [hold:ten_year_history.2020_2025.average_organic_growth_pct]. This engine has never had an organic floor above zero in real terms; 2023 to 2025 was the exception.

Above the line, only Topicus: 5 percent organic in FY2024, 4 in FY2025, 4 in Q2 2026 [hold:listed_subsidiaries.topicus.organic_growth_pct:FY2024] [hold:listed_subsidiaries.topicus.organic_growth_pct:FY2025] [hold:listed_subsidiaries.topicus.organic_growth_pct:Q2 2026], on a base roughly 70 percent maintenance and recurring [hold:listed_subsidiaries.topicus.maintenance_share_of_revenue]. Below it, Lumine: 0 percent FX adjusted in FY2023, negative 9 in FY2024, 1 in FY2025, negative 2 in Q1 2026 [hold:listed_subsidiaries.lumine.organic_growth_pct:FY2023] [holdings:lumine:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:FY2024] [holdings:lumine:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:FY2025] [holdings:lumine:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:Q1 2026]. Four years without a real gain.

Unknown, everything else. Vela's only organic figures were parent numbers misattributed at intake and were nulled [holdings:vela:organic_growth_pct]. Volaris, Harris, Jonas, Perseus and Omegro disclose none, only commentary [holdings:volaris:organic_growth_commentary] [holdings:omegro:gaps]. That is most of the company by revenue.

The counterweight is the base. Maintenance and recurring revenue rose from 71.2 percent of revenue in FY2023 to 76.6 percent in H1 2026 [metrics:maintenance_share_of_revenue:FY2023] [metrics:maintenance_share_of_revenue:H1 2026], and FY2025 maintenance organic growth was 6 percent [ledger:unmapped:FY2025 maintenance and recurring revenue organic growth]. The base is not shrinking in dollars, it is failing to outgrow prices. No inflation series exists in any file, so real organic growth is read off the nominal sign, and at 1 percent FX adjusted that sign is negative.

Verdict: Fails. Evidence weight: moderate for the parent and the listed subsidiaries, none for the private groups.

## 3. Decentralisation as moat

Founder era leaders still in post: Barry Symons at Jonas since 2007 after eleven years at Constellation, latterly as CFO [holdings:jonas:leadership], David Nyland at Lumine since 2014 [holdings:lumine:leadership], Robin van Poelje at Topicus since 2021 [holdings:topicus:leadership].

Successions completed. Harris moved Jeff Bender to Executive Chairman and Jean Soucy to Group CEO in June 2024 [holdings:harris:leadership]. Perseus lost founding CEO Dexter Salna at the end of 2023, who stayed as Chairman [holdings:perseus:leadership]. Volaris named Mike Dufton CEO in January 2024, Mark Miller moving to Executive Chairman and then to Constellation President in late 2025 [holdings:volaris:leadership]. Topicus handed over in November 2021 [stage1:topicus:https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2021/11/25/2341279/0/en/Robin-van-Poelje-named-]. Two dates conflict with this stage's spec, which lists Volaris at 2025.

Allocators exported. Dufton founded Vencora inside Volaris in 2022 before taking the group [holdings:volaris:leadership]. Troy O'Connor came in through the 2017 Smartrak acquisition and co runs Omegro [holdings:omegro:leadership]. Damian McKay came in through the 2015 Datamine acquisition and has run Vela since 2020 [holdings:vela:leadership]. Bonnie Wilhelm moved from Constellation HomeBuilder finance to Perseus co President [holdings:perseus:leadership]. New units: Topicus in 2021 and Lumine in February 2023 as listed spin outs, Vencora and CORA in November 2022, Omegro in June 2024 [holdings:topicus:status_note] [holdings:lumine:status_note] [holdings:omegro:status_note] [holdings:jonas:status_note]. Five in four years, each led from inside.

Against. Role doubling suggests a bench thinner than the chart: Dufton runs Volaris and Vencora, Miller holds Volaris Executive Chairman, Constellation President and the Lumine board chair at once, and Matt Otchet runs both a Jonas group and CORA [holdings:volaris:leadership] [holdings:lumine:leadership] [holdings:jonas:leadership]. Perseus has no publicly named successor CEO two years on [holdings:perseus:gaps]. The structure produces allocators faster than it consumes them, but the count is the only evidence: no tenures, no record of those who left.

Verdict: Holds with conditions. Evidence weight: moderate.

## 4. AI exposure by vertical

On the first report's four layers, least exposed are Harris and Topicus, which also carry the strongest organic evidence. That pairing is a real moat, not an asserted one. Harris sits almost wholly in the regulated system of record layer, in public sector, healthcare, utilities, public safety and justice [holdings:harris:verticals], including Altera Digital Health, bought in 2022 for GBP 526 million [holdings:harris:notable_businesses]. Topicus is the European equivalent in education, healthcare, social services and government [holdings:topicus:verticals].

Most exposed is Jonas, in clubs, fitness, hospitality, foodservice, marina and liquor point of sale [holdings:jonas:verticals]. Almost no regulatory anchor, and many niches small enough that collapsing build cost lets a two person team enter, the layer the first report called at risk. Omegro is next on crowded ERP, CRM, warehousing and fleet, though its January 2026 refocus onto Enterprise Asset Management moves up the stack [holdings:omegro:status_note]. Vela's travel cluster sits in embedded workflow [stage1:vela:https://velasoftwaregroup.com/newsletter-q1-25-2024-year-in-review/]. Perseus is split, mortgage origination a regulated record while dealer websites are not [holdings:perseus:verticals], and its Hyderabad centre is a services heavy build [holdings:perseus:ai_statements]. Volaris is a barbell of transit, justice and aviation against MarTech and retail [holdings:volaris:verticals].

Weighting is where this test breaks. Jonas is 8.3 percent of FY2024 revenue and 9.4 percent of headcount [hold:concentration.revenue_share.jonas:FY2024] [hold:concentration.headcount_share.jonas:~2024], Volaris 26.1 percent of FY2025 revenue on a flagged third party series [hold:concentration.revenue_share.volaris:FY2025], Lumine 6.6 percent [hold:concentration.revenue_share.lumine:FY2025]. Harris cannot be placed: 40.0 percent of approximate parent headcount in 2024 [hold:concentration.headcount_share.harris:~2024] against a third party 2026 revenue figure with no parent FY2026 revenue to divide by [hold:concentration.revenue_share.harris:FY2026]. Revenue share is null for Perseus, Vela, Topicus and Omegro [hold:concentration.revenue_share.perseus] and business count share null everywhere [hold:concentration.business_count_share.volaris]. The most exposed group is roughly 8 to 9 percent of the whole; the least exposed cannot be weighted.

Verdict: Holds with conditions. Evidence weight: thin.

## 5. Listed subsidiaries

What CSU owns. Topicus: 30.35 percent of the economics fully diluted, all the preferred shares, and one super voting share carrying 50.1 percent of votes [hold:look_through.topicus.economic_interest_pct] [holdings:topicus:csu_voting_control]. That is a January 2021 spin out figure with nothing more recent found, and Topicus has issued since. Lumine: 61.40 percent equity at 31 March 2026, all subordinate voting shares after the March 2024 conversion, a 38.60 percent minority, controlled and consolidated [hold:look_through.lumine.economic_interest_pct] [holdings:lumine:csu_voting_control].

What it is worth. Topicus look through value is CAD 2,546 million, 3.91 percent of CSU market cap [hold:look_through.topicus.look_through_value_native] [hold:look_through.share_of_csu_market_cap.topicus.share_of_csu_market_cap_reported_cad:single]. Lumine is CAD 3,456 to 3,938 million on two conflicting September 2026 market caps, 5.30 to 6.04 percent [hold:look_through.lumine.look_through_value_native_low] [hold:look_through.lumine.look_through_value_native_high] [hold:look_through.share_of_csu_market_cap.lumine.share_of_csu_market_cap_reported_cad:low] [hold:look_through.share_of_csu_market_cap.lumine.share_of_csu_market_cap_reported_cad:high]. Combined that is 9.21 to 9.95 percent, computed here from 3.91 plus 5.30 and 3.91 plus 6.04, leaving CAD 58,699 to 59,181 million implied for the private groups [hold:look_through.implied_value_private_groups.using_reported_cad_market_cap:with_lumine_high] [hold:look_through.implied_value_private_groups.using_reported_cad_market_cap:with_lumine_low].

The FX caveat is not cosmetic. No FX rate was sourced at intake, so both USD look through values are null and the shares above are CAD against CAD [hold:look_through.topicus.usd_value] [hold:look_through.lumine.usd_value]. The denominator conflicts, USD 47,950 million against CAD 65,183 million [ledger:market_cap_usd:2026-09] [ledger:market_cap_cad:2026-09-08], a derived 1.3594 against 1.2497 implied by the first report's entry [metrics:post_review:usdcad_derived] [metrics:post_review:implied_usdcad_in_report_entry]. Across USDCAD 1.25 to 1.3594 those shares widen, putting the combined stake at 9 to 11 percent [hold:look_through.share_of_csu_market_cap.topicus.share_of_csu_market_cap_fx_sensitivity] [hold:look_through.share_of_csu_market_cap.lumine.share_of_csu_market_cap_fx_sensitivity].

Created or leaked. Created: acquisition currency that never touches CSU shares, most visibly USD 222 million of Lumine Special Shares in the USD 504 million WideOrbit price [holdings:lumine:notable_businesses]; separate balance sheets, Topicus net debt EUR 127 million against Lumine net cash USD 142 million [hold:listed_subsidiaries.topicus.net_debt:Q2 2026] [hold:listed_subsidiaries.lumine.net_debt:FY2025]; and retention, both listed CEOs being founders who stayed. Leaked: WideOrbit holders took 13.83 percent of Lumine fully diluted [stage1:lumine:https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2023/02/22/2613833/0/en/Constellation-Software-], a stage1 only figure, and Topicus at 30.35 percent economic means most of its FY2025 FCFA2S of EUR 218.7 million goes to minorities [holdings:topicus:fcfa2s:FY2025]. The model creates more than it leaks, but it externalised the two best disclosed assets, leaving the case on CAD 59 billion of groups that disclose nothing.

Verdict: Holds with conditions. Evidence weight: moderate.
