# Constellation Software: Nineteen Years on the Record

## Executive Summary

(Fable 5.1 writes this section at Stage 6.)

## The Company in One Page

Constellation Software buys vertical market software businesses, software written for one specific industry rather than for everyone, and keeps them. It does not build from scratch and does not sell what it buys. The company runs as a small number of largely autonomous operating groups, each holding dozens to hundreds of these businesses, capital allocation pushed down to managers close to the customer. The lineage runs deep: Trapeze, the first acquisition in 1995, seeded the group later named Volaris in 2011 [event:2011:operating_group]; Harris dates to 1976; Jonas has built in clubs and hospitality software since 2003 [stage1:years_2007_2011.json:https://jonassoftware.com/20-years-of-jonas]; Friedman, folded into Harris, dates to 1999 [stage1:years_2007_2011.json:https://mergr.com/transaction/constellation-software-acquires-friedman]. Constellation listed on the TSX on 17 May 2006 at C$17.00 [event:2006-05-17:ipo].

The model in five sentences. Find small, profitable software businesses with sticky, recurring revenue in niches too specific for most acquirers to notice. Buy them for cash, never for stock, and never sell what has been bought. Leave existing management running the business day to day, judged on return on invested capital rather than growth for its own sake. Take almost all of the cash the group throws off and put it into buying the next several hundred businesses like it, rather than paying it out as dividends. Hold the resulting cash flow per share, not the share price, as the thing meant to compound, since the share count essentially never moves.

That last sentence rests on a labelled assumption: the share count is carried forward at 21.19 million from FY2006 through FY2022 on two third party statements that it has been unchanged since the IPO, with only FY2025 confirmed from a company filing [hist:shares_outstanding:FY2006] [hist:shares_outstanding:FY2025]. Every per share figure in this report therefore equals its corresponding total figure exactly; this is stated once here and once more under Gaps and Data Limitations.

## Four Eras

The share count is a labelled assumption of 21.19 million [hist:shares_outstanding:FY2006], carried forward from FY2006 to FY2022 on two third party statements that it is unchanged since the IPO, with only FY2025 company sourced [hist:shares_outstanding:FY2025]. Because the denominator never moves, every per share CAGR below equals its total CAGR exactly [hm:eras:revenue_per_share_cagr_pct:FY2007 to FY2025], an artefact of that assumption rather than a finding.

### Era 1: Listing and proving the model, 2006 to 2011

Constellation listed at C$17.00 on 17 May 2006 [event:2006-05-17:ipo], carrying a public test: Net Revenues and Adjusted EBITDA per share above 20 percent a year, 2006 to 2010 [event:2006:ipo_growth_target].

Revenue went 243.02, third party, to 773 USD millions [hist:revenue:FY2007] [hist:revenue:FY2011], 33.55 percent a year, the fastest era [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2007 to FY2011], on organic growth averaging negative 5.67 percent [hm:eras:avg_organic_growth_pct:FY2007 to FY2011]. ROIC was 24 and 25 percent in FY2009 and FY2010 [hist:roic_pct:FY2009] [hist:roic_pct:FY2010], the only two such figures on record. Deployment and cash are close to unrecorded: only FY2008 shows spend, about 94 across seventeen deals [hist:acquisitions_cash_spent:FY2008] [hist:acquisition_count:FY2008], and adjusted net income of 62 and 140 in FY2009 and FY2011 [hist:adjusted_net_income:FY2009] [hist:adjusted_net_income:FY2011] leaves no era cash CAGR [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2007 to FY2011]. On 4 April 2011 the board put the company up for sale, "The Process" [event:2011-04-04:strategic_review_2011].

Verdict: not assessable against its own test. FY2006 revenue and Adjusted EBITDA are absent and the share count is an assumption, so both per share CAGRs and the pass or fail are null [hm:ipo_targets:met_target:FY2006 to FY2010]; management called 16 percent 2007 revenue growth challenging but achievable, citing 33 percent EBITDA growth [event:2007:ipo_growth_target_progress] [hist:revenue_growth_pct:FY2007], and FY2010 grew 44 percent [hist:revenue_growth_pct:FY2010]. Third party commentary claims the target was exceeded, unconfirmed [stage1:years_2007_2011.json:https://www.csisoftware.com/docs/default-source/investor-relations/presidents-letter/shareholder-letter---2010.pdf].

### Era 2: Scaling and returning capital, 2012 to 2016

The aim was to stay independent: the review ended without a sale, and the board adopted a true USD 1.00 quarterly dividend from Q1 2012, after about 2 per share for 2011 [event:2012:strategic_review_outcome] [hist:dividends_per_share_regular:FY2011] [hist:dividends_per_share_regular:FY2012]. Revenue went 891.2 to 2,125.1 USD millions, 24.26 percent a year [hist:revenue:FY2012] [hist:revenue:FY2016] [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2012 to FY2016]; adjusted net income went 172 to 395.0, 23.10 percent a year on the same basis [hist:adjusted_net_income:FY2012] [hist:adjusted_net_income:FY2016] [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2012 to FY2016], the only clean same basis pair in the record. Net income sat flat at 93 in FY2012 and FY2013 [hist:net_income_attributable:FY2012] [hist:net_income_attributable:FY2013] against FY2013 cash conversion of 2.37 times [hm:annual:cash_conversion_ratio:FY2013], the first sign amortisation had decoupled earnings from cash. Organic averaged 2.2 percent, largely currency in the slower years [hm:eras:avg_organic_growth_pct:FY2012 to FY2016] [hist:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:FY2015] [hist:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:FY2016]. Deployment was lumpy, 558 on 30 deals in FY2013 then 115 on 23 in FY2014 [hist:acquisitions_cash_spent:FY2013] [hist:acquisition_count:FY2013] [hist:acquisitions_cash_spent:FY2014], totalling 1,099.9 at 96.62 percent of CFO, the highest reinvestment intensity of any era [hm:eras:total_acquisitions_spent:FY2012 to FY2016] [hm:eras:avg_acquisitions_to_cfo_pct:FY2012 to FY2016]. The defining act was twofold: fixing the dividend rather than raising it [hm:eras:total_dividends_per_share:FY2012 to FY2016], and Total Specific Solutions, agreed 16 December 2013 for about EUR 240 million, the largest deal to date, funded with a one year USD 350 million term loan [event:2013-12-16:acquisition_above_100m].

Verdict: delivered. Revenue and the cash proxy both compounded above 23 percent [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2012 to FY2016] [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2012 to FY2016] and the company stayed independent; the blemish is organic growth at 2.2 percent, negative in one year [hm:eras:avg_organic_growth_pct:FY2012 to FY2016].

### Era 3: Larger deals and looser hurdles, 2017 to 2021

The aim was to deploy a cash pile faster than the pipeline could absorb, and when that failed, hand the excess back once. Revenue went 2,479, a third party approximation, to 5,106, 19.80 percent a year, the slowest era [hist:revenue:FY2017] [hist:revenue:FY2021] [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2017 to FY2021]. Cash from operations went 528 to 1,300, 25.26 percent a year [hist:cash_from_operations:FY2017] [hist:cash_from_operations:FY2021] [hm:eras:cfo_cagr_pct:FY2017 to FY2021]. No cash metric CAGR exists here and none can: the era opens on adjusted net income of 463 and closes on FCFA2S of 883 [hist:adjusted_net_income:FY2017] [hist:fcfa2s:FY2021] [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2017 to FY2021], FCFA2S adopted only with the Q3 2019 release [event:2019-10-31:fcfa2s_introduced]; the metrics file prints a bridging per share figure of 17.52 percent that must not be used [hm:eras:cash_metric_per_share_cagr_pct:FY2017 to FY2021]. Organic averaged 1.8 percent [hm:eras:avg_organic_growth_pct:FY2017 to FY2021]; COVID FY2020 was negative 3 percent reported and after FX [hist:organic_growth_fx_adj_pct:FY2020]. Spend averaged 76.55 percent of CFO [hm:eras:avg_acquisitions_to_cfo_pct:FY2017 to FY2021], FY2020 the extreme at FCFA2S of 989 against acquisitions of only 179, leaving 810 unspent [hist:fcfa2s:FY2020] [hist:acquisitions_cash_spent:FY2020] [hm:annual:cash_after_acquisitions:FY2020].

Three decisions, all about cash the machine could not eat: the USD 20.00 special dividend of 14 February 2019, declared on the view that capital exceeded needs and never repeated [event:2019-02-14:special_dividend] [hist:dividends_per_share_special:FY2018]; the February 2021 letter lowering the hurdle on large deals, since brokers had included Constellation in only about 16 percent of large VMS sale processes [event:2021-02:hurdle_rate_change]; and the Topicus spin out of 5 January 2021 [event:2021-01-05:spin_out]. Leonard's 2018 letter was his last as a matter of course [event:2018:president_letter_policy_change].

Verdict: mixed. Operating cash compounded at 25.26 percent [hm:eras:cfo_cagr_pct:FY2017 to FY2021] and the company admitted it could not deploy the result; growing by buying produced the slowest revenue CAGR of the four, and the fix was to lower the price of admission.

### Era 4: Big deals and succession, 2022 to 2025

The aim was to spend at the new large deal hurdle, spin out what can stand alone, and hand the company over. Revenue went 6,622 to 11,623 USD millions, 20.63 percent a year [hist:revenue:FY2022] [hist:revenue:FY2025] [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2022 to FY2025]. FCFA2S went 853 to 1,683, 25.42 percent a year on a clean single basis, the only usable cash CAGR besides era 2's [hist:fcfa2s:FY2022] [hist:fcfa2s:FY2025] [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2022 to FY2025] [metrics:fcfa2s_cagr_3yr:FY2022-FY2025], CFO compounding faster still at 28.19 percent [hm:eras:cfo_cagr_pct:FY2022 to FY2025]. Net income is not a usable read: net margin was 7.73 percent in FY2022 and 4.41 in FY2025 against FY2025 cash conversion of 5.34 times [hm:annual:net_margin_pct:FY2022] [hm:annual:net_margin_pct:FY2025] [hm:annual:cash_conversion_ratio:FY2025]. Organic averaged 2.5 percent, the best era average and still barely above nothing [hm:eras:avg_organic_growth_pct:FY2022 to FY2025]. Deployment is the signature: spend totalled 5,703 [hm:eras:total_acquisitions_spent:FY2022 to FY2025], exceeding the previous three eras combined, 94 plus 1,099.9 plus 2,807 equals 4,000.9, computed here [hm:eras:total_acquisitions_spent:FY2007 to FY2011] [hm:eras:total_acquisitions_spent:FY2012 to FY2016] [hm:eras:total_acquisitions_spent:FY2017 to FY2021].

Two deals of unprecedented size defined the era: Allscripts Hospitals and Large Physician Practices for up to USD 700 million in March 2022 [event:2022-03-02:acquisition_above_100m], and Black Knight's Empower and Optimal Blue for about USD 700 million in September 2023 [event:2023-09-15:acquisition_above_100m]. The Lumine spin out with WideOrbit followed in February 2023 [event:2023-02-22:spin_out]. Then succession: Leonard resigned on 25 September 2025 citing health, Mark Miller appointed, shares falling 5.5 percent [event:2025-09-25:leadership_change].

Verdict: delivered on its stated aim, failed the underlying one. The 2021 large deal ambition was met, twice, cash per share compounding at 25.42 percent [hm:eras:cash_metric_per_share_cagr_pct:FY2022 to FY2025]. But the prior report finds ROIC falling from 14.8 percent in 2025 to 10.97 at June 2026 against a stated 25 percent hurdle [ledger:unmapped:ROIC:2025, June 2026, TTM 2026] [ledger:unmapped:Hurdle rate IRR target:2024-2026]. Bigger deals bought the growth; they did not restore the return.

### What stayed constant across all four

Constant: the share count and the dividend, flat throughout, no dilution, no buyback, no equity issued for deals, dividend at USD 1.00 a quarter from Q1 2012, never raised [hist:dividends_per_share_regular:FY2013] [hist:dividends_per_share_regular:FY2025], plus one special ever, 80.00 per share over sixteen years [hm:eras:total_dividends_per_share:FY2007 to FY2025]; organic growth, never above a 0.76 percent full window average [hm:eras:avg_organic_growth_pct:FY2007 to FY2025]; acquisitions, absorbing 83.05 percent of CFO over the twelve years both exist, likely understated given six years with no spend figure [hm:eras:avg_acquisitions_to_cfo_pct:FY2007 to FY2025] [hm:eras:total_acquisitions_spent:FY2007 to FY2025]; and revenue, compounding at 23.97 percent full window with no down year [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2007 to FY2025].

Not constant: the cash yardstick, changed from adjusted net income to FCFA2S, no full window cash CAGR [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2007 to FY2025]; disclosure, narrowed to last adjusted EBITA FY2018 [hist:adjusted_ebita:FY2018] and last ROIC FY2010 [hist:roic_pct:FY2010]; and returns, falling while spending rose. Year end share prices are gap for every fiscal year [hm:eras:share_price_cagr_cad_pct:FY2007 to FY2025], covered under Per Share Record and Total Return.

It is an acquisition engine, not a profit engine. Reported profit is the least informative line in the file: amortization exceeded net income in both years it can be measured. ## What the Record Says

1. **Revenue compounded for nineteen years without a single down year, at a rate few businesses sustain this long.** The full window CAGR is 23.97 percent [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2007 to FY2025], built from era CAGRs of 33.55, 24.26, 19.80 and 20.63 percent that never inverted [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2007 to FY2011] [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2012 to FY2016] [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2017 to FY2021] [hm:eras:revenue_cagr_pct:FY2022 to FY2025], the single weakest year, FY2015, still growing 10.13 percent [hm:annual:revenue_growth_pct:FY2015].

2. **Organic growth never carried the company, in any era.** The full window average is 0.76 percent across seventeen of the nineteen years with a reading [hm:eras:avg_organic_growth_pct:FY2007 to FY2025], and the best era average, 2.5 percent in FY2022 to FY2025, is still barely above nothing [hm:eras:avg_organic_growth_pct:FY2022 to FY2025]. The best single year, FY2021 at 7 percent [hist:organic_growth_pct:FY2021], was never repeated; every dollar of the top line above that thin organic layer was bought.

3. **Acquisitions absorbed most of the cash the business generated, and probably more than the record shows.** Over the twelve years carrying both figures, acquisitions ran 83.05 percent of cash from operations [hm:eras:avg_acquisitions_to_cfo_pct:FY2007 to FY2025], with era averages of 96.62, 76.55 and 75.98 percent [hm:eras:avg_acquisitions_to_cfo_pct:FY2012 to FY2016] [hm:eras:avg_acquisitions_to_cfo_pct:FY2017 to FY2021] [hm:eras:avg_acquisitions_to_cfo_pct:FY2022 to FY2025]. Six of the nineteen years carry no acquisition spend figure at all, so the true full window figure is at least this high and probably higher.

4. **The cash yardstick changed mid record, making a clean nineteen year cash CAGR impossible.** Adjusted net income served as the cash proxy through FY2017; FCFA2S replaced it from FY2018 [event:2019-10-31:fcfa2s_introduced]. The two clean CAGRs on file are 23.10 percent FY2012 to FY2016 [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2012 to FY2016] and 25.42 percent FY2022 to FY2025 [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2022 to FY2025]; the FY2017 to FY2021 era and full window have no valid cash CAGR, and a bridging figure is an error, not a finding [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2017 to FY2021] [hm:eras:cash_metric_cagr_pct:FY2007 to FY2025].

5. **The return on capital that would justify the reinvestment rate was disclosed twice in nineteen years, both before 2011.** ROIC of 24 and 25 percent in FY2009 and FY2010 [hist:roic_pct:FY2009] [hist:roic_pct:FY2010] are the only figures on file; with acquisitions gap in both years, the reinvestment times return identity cannot be closed from primary sources [hm:reinvestment:implied_per_share_growth_pct:FY2009] [hm:reinvestment:implied_per_share_growth_pct:FY2013].

6. **The capital return policy has been retention dressed as a dividend, broken exactly once.** The regular dividend fixed at USD 1.00 a quarter since Q1 2012 has never been raised [hist:dividends_per_share_regular:FY2013] [hist:dividends_per_share_regular:FY2025], totalling 80.00 per share across sixteen years [hm:eras:total_dividends_per_share:FY2007 to FY2025]. The one break was the USD 20.00 special declared with the FY2018 results, capital in excess of needs [event:2019-02-14:special_dividend] [hist:dividends_per_share_special:FY2018], never repeated since.

7. **Six of twelve measurable years spent more on acquisitions and dividends than the year generated, funded by debt and vendor paper, not equity.** Cash after acquisitions and dividends was negative in FY2013, FY2018, FY2019, FY2021, FY2022 and FY2024 [hm:annual:cash_after_acquisitions_and_dividends:FY2013] [hm:annual:cash_after_acquisitions_and_dividends:FY2018] [hm:annual:cash_after_acquisitions_and_dividends:FY2019] [hm:annual:cash_after_acquisitions_and_dividends:FY2021] [hm:annual:cash_after_acquisitions_and_dividends:FY2022] [hm:annual:cash_after_acquisitions_and_dividends:FY2024]. The largest deals were financed accordingly: a one year USD 350 million term loan for TSS in FY2013 [event:2013-12-16:acquisition_above_100m], and a 500 million promissory note behind the FY2023 Black Knight purchase [event:2023-09-15:acquisition_above_100m]. No year shows equity issued for a deal.

8. **Every shareholder return figure in this record is either third party or rests on a single price point, because year end share prices were never recoverable.** Year end CAD share price is gap for all nineteen fiscal years despite dedicated search passes in every intake window [hist:share_price_cad_year_end:FY2025].
