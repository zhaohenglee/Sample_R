What this adds to the two earlier reports. The first said Not Buy at today's price. The second said Hold with conditions on the engine. This one shows the engine's record is real, long, and consistent on the lines that matter, cash and per share value, and that its weakness is not new: organic growth was negative for the first five years on file and has averaged under 1 percent since. The history gives no precedent for that combination lasting, because the company never before deployed this much at returns this far below its hurdle.

The Company in One Page

Constellation Software buys vertical market software businesses, software written for one specific industry rather than for everyone, and keeps them. It does not build from scratch and does not sell what it buys. The company runs as a small number of largely autonomous operating groups, each holding dozens to hundreds of these businesses, capital allocation pushed down to managers close to the customer. The model in five sentences. Find small, profitable software businesses with sticky, recurring revenue in niches too specific for most acquirers to notice. Buy them for cash, never for stock, and never sell what has been bought. Leave existing management running the business day to day, judged on return on invested capital rather than growth for its own sake. Take almost all of the cash the group throws off and put it into buying the next several hundred businesses like it, rather than paying it out as dividends. Hold the resulting cash flow per share, not the share price, as the thing meant to compound, since the share count essentially never moves.
