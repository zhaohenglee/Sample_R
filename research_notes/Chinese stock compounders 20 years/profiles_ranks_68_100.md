## Core business and ROE record of each company (2006 to 2025)

### Takeaway
Of the 33 companies, 13 averaged 15 percent or more over 2016 to 2025, only 7 cleared 15 percent in at least 8 of those 10 years, and 8 averaged under 10 percent. ROE for A shares is the weighted average ROE as reported (Eastmoney `ROEJQ`); for Hong Kong names it is net income attributable over average equity (Eastmoney `ROE_AVG`). Detailed lines follow, ordered by rank.

### Cited Findings
**Definitions and method.** A shares: weighted average ROE (加权平均净资产收益率) from the Eastmoney annual indicator table, which carries the latest published (possibly restated) figure for each year; Yili and Wanhua use the existing `roe_prefetch.csv` series (Sina first published figure, Eastmoney fallback) so they match the other notes. Hong Kong: net profit attributable divided by average of opening and closing parent equity (`ROE_AVG`; the prefetch note verified this formula on Tencent 2025). "ge15" = number of years at or above 15 percent in 2016 to 2025; "min" is over 2006 to 2025 years with data. Eastmoney restated values can differ from first published ones (the prefetch note found Wanhua 2017 at 85.9 on Eastmoney versus 50.7 first published); this was not checked per company except where flagged — [compounder_fundamentals_prefetch.md](file:///home/user/Sample_R/research_notes/Chinese stock compounders 20 years/compounder_fundamentals_prefetch.md); [roe_prefetch.csv](file:///home/user/Sample_R/research_notes/Chinese stock compounders 20 years/roe_prefetch.csv)

## Was the ROE sustainable, and what drove the return where it was not

### Takeaway
Verdicts across the 33: yes 4, partly 14, no 15. Sustained ROE (brand, captive scale, cost position) is rare in this band; most names are commodity or capex cycle stocks, asset injection or leverage stories, or low ROE stocks whose return came from earnings growth from a tiny base and re-rating. The reasons below are my judgments from the cited figures.

### Inferences
- The names with durable ROE (verdict yes) rest on a consumer brand with a small equity base (Supor, Lao Feng Xiang, Yili) or a global oligopoly position (Wanhua). Wanhua (12.4) and Lao Feng Xiang (13.6) were below 15 in 2025, so none is immune to cycles.
- The partly group falls into three kinds: cost leaders with price cycles (Xinyi Glass, Hualu-Hengsheng, Longsheng), companies tied to a state or group parent (Huayu, Baosight, Sichuan Road & Bridge, Brilliance), and leverage driven names (Hisense Home Appliances, Zijin Longking, Fuling Electric).
- For the no group the return mostly came from earnings growth off a very low or loss making base and from re-rating (Grinm, Triumph, NBTM, Shibao, Haohua, Fenghua), from one commodity or product spike (Keda, HEC, TBEA, Yankuang, CGN Mining) or from asset injection and shell restructuring (Joyson, Haohua, Huayu, Fuling).
- For the 16 names with a 2016 to 2025 average ROE under 13 percent, ROE does not explain the 20 year CAGR; their returns came from the other drivers listed in the verdict lines.

### Gaps
- Causes not found in sources: the 2021 one off gain at Zhejiang Dong Ri; why Longsheng's dye gross margin fell in 2023; share price drivers for Shibao, Fenghua 2018, Hisense Visual 2011 and Haohua; the 2018 asset injection details for AVIC Aviation High-Technology. These are not guessed in the verdict lines.
- Revenue figures for Hong Kong names come from press or HKEX summary pages (iTiger, HKEX), not from annual report PDFs. The CGN Mining and Brilliance descriptions rest on secondary sources (press and aggregator summaries).
- Yili and Wanhua use the prefetch (first published, Sina) ROE; the other A shares use Eastmoney's latest published figures, so the bases are not identical. Eastmoney 2025 data are from annual reports published March to April 2026.
- Weighted ROE for A shares and net income over average equity for Hong Kong are different definitions; PICC P&C, Great Wall and Xinyi Glass are on the Hong Kong basis, Yankuang is on the H share basis (the A share would give slightly different figures).
- Hisense Home Appliances also has an H share (formerly 0921.HK); it is not in the HK screen, so no dual listing choice was made and it was not investigated further.
