## Debt: instruments, maturity wall, revolver, covenants, 2025 to 2026 refinancing

### Takeaway
The debt stack is essentially one instrument: $700M of 10.625% senior secured notes due January 15, 2031, issued June 2025 to refinance the $485M 6.875% secured notes due 2027. A $125M ABL revolver (undrawn) runs to June 2030. No significant maturity before 2031 per management, so near term bankruptcy risk is a liquidity and pension cash question, not a maturity wall.

### Cited Findings
- Issued $700M of 10.625% senior secured notes due January 15, 2031 (144A/Reg S); offering closed June 27, 2025 — [10-K FY2025 via search summary](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746838/000074683826000006/uis-20251231.htm); [closing release](https://wwwprod12.unisys.com/siteassets/collateral/news/closing-private-offering-senior-secured-notes-062725.pdf)
- Proceeds refinanced the $485M 6.875% senior secured notes due 2027; the remainder went to the pension deficit/postretirement and general corporate purposes. About $200M of the $250M discretionary 2025 US pension contribution came from the note proceeds, $50M from cash — [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746838/000074683826000006/uis-20251231.htm)
- Original 2027 notes were issued in 2020 as a $485M secured offering to fund US pension plans — [Unisys release](https://unisys.com/news-release/unisys-announces-closing-of-485-million-private-offering-of-senior-secured-notes-net-proceeds-to-be-used-to-fund-us-pension-plans)
- Long term debt $724.0M at March 31, 2026; undrawn $125M ABL; "no significant debt maturities until 2031" — [1Q26 release summary](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/unisys-announces-1q26-results-2026-05-05)
- Amended and Restated ABL: up to $125.0M of revolving loans and letters of credit (LC sublimit $40.0M), uncommitted accordion to $155.0M; maturity extended from October 2027 to June 2030. At December 31, 2025: no borrowings, $13.5M LCs, availability $92.2M net of LCs (borrowing base on receivables) — [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746838/000074683826000006/uis-20251231.htm)
- Springing maturity: ABL matures 91 days before any date on which US pension contributions above $100.0M are required, unless conditions are met including liquidity above $130M and a pro forma fixed charge coverage test — [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/746838/000074683826000006/uis-20251231.htm)
- 1Q26 investor presentation lists total debt $1,198.2M, estimated global net pension deficit $448.5M (Dec 31, 2025), net leverage 2.9x and 1.3x excluding the pension deficit. The search tool flagged this as a jumbled extraction — [1Q26 presentation](https://www.unisys.com/siteassets/investor-relations/earnings-files/2026/1q-2026-presentation.pdf)
- Management says it expects to meet all debt covenants and liquidity needs for at least the next 12 months (Quartr summary of Q2 2026) — [Quartr](https://quartr.com/events/unisys-corporation-uis-q2-2026_ofaJGMo3)

### Inferences

- With the 2027 notes gone and revolver extended, the springing maturity (US pension contributions above $100M in a year) is the main structural link between pension and the revolver. Disclosed US contributions are about $47M in 2026, so it is not tripped on current disclosure.

## Precedent: Unisys's earlier distress and resolution

### Takeaway
Unisys was widely expected to file for bankruptcy around 1989 to 1991 after the Burroughs and Sperry merger left heavy debt; it avoided Chapter 11 by cutting debt from $4B to $1.1B by 1993, then needed a second cleanup under Weinbach from 1997. The "early 2000s" distress was not found in sources; the second episode found is 1997 to 2000.

### Cited Findings
- From 1989 to 1991 Unisys lost more than $2B and many expected a bankruptcy filing; debt was reduced from $4B (1989) to $1.1B (1993) — [Washington Technology 1994](https://washingtontechnology.com/1994/10/unisys-troubling-transformation/325534)
- Debt rose again in 1994 to 1996 amid declining mainframe revenue; Weinbach became Chairman, President and CEO in September 1997 with $2.3B of debt; by 2000 he had cut corporate debt by $1.2B, ahead of schedule ("We never sold any assets to pay off debt"); operating cash flow turned positive for the first time in about ten years by 1998; a 1998 filing shows about $1.1B of one time charges — [Route Fifty/GCN 2003](https://route-fifty.com/digital-government/2003/10/industry-executive-of-the-year-weinbachs-sharpened-focus-revived-unisys/294273); [Washington Technology 1998](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/1998/05/weinbach-returns-unisys-to-stardom/331131/); [SEC filing 1997](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000746838/000089322097001769/0000893220-97-001769.txt/seq-2)

### Inferences
- Both prior episodes were resolved out of court via cost cuts, refocus and debt paydown, not Chapter 11; the 2020s pension annuitization program is the current analog (liability reduction without cash). This is an inference from limited sources.
