## 1. How do Moody's and S&P treat an unfunded pension for a company like Unisys, and what leverage, coverage and liquidity thresholds separate B from CCC? (cash flow and coverage frameworks)

### Takeaway
Both agencies add the pension shortfall to debt. Moody's standard adjustment counts the gross shortfall (PBO minus plan assets at fair value) as debt (per a methodology excerpt, snippet level). I could not retrieve the numeric B vs CCC leverage, coverage or liquidity thresholds from either agency's business services or technology methodology, so they are a gap. What is documented is the qualitative CCC line (vulnerable, dependent on favorable conditions, roughly 50% default likelihood) and how Unisys's own pension-inclusive leverage is reported (2.8x to 3.1x net, versus 1.5x excluding pension).

### Cited Findings
Pension treatment by the agencies
- Moody's standard adjustments for non-financial corporates list underfunded and unfunded defined benefit pensions as the first of nine adjustment areas; the quoted method is "recognizes the gross underfunded pension obligation (PBO – Fair Market Value of assets) as debt, and removes any remaining intangible pension assets and liabilities" — [IPE, Moody's clarifies ratings for unfunded pensions](https://www.ipe.com/moodys-clarifies-ratings-for-unfunded-pensions/940.article) and a business-case paper reproducing the excerpt, [jo.aabri.com](https://jo.aabri.com/manuscripts/213373.pdf) (snippet level; the excerpt may be from the IFRS-oriented adjustment note, so check it against the US GAAP version).
- Moody's applies "standard pension, lease and finance company adjustments" in practice: for Xerox it cited debt/EBITDA of about 6x at 31 Dec 2025 including those adjustments, about 9x without the finance company adjustment — [Action Intell, Moody's downgrades Xerox](https://www.action-intell.com/2026/03/16/moodys-downgrades-xerox-debt-further-into-junk-territory/) (snippet level).
- S&P's current criteria are "Corporate Methodology: Ratios And Adjustments", published 1 April 2019, which organizes adjustments under four principles and still appears in 2025 sector methodologies — [S&P criteria table of contents](https://spglobal.com/ratings/en/research/articles/200604-table-of-contents-s-p-global-ratings-corporate-criteria-5475113) and [Maalot copy of S&P material](https://www.maalot.co.il/Publications/MT20191103145619.PDF) (snippet level). I did not retrieve the pension paragraph or the after-tax treatment.
- Moody's published business and consumer services methodology cited as of November 2021 in a periodic review notice — [Kowatek blog repost of Moody's notice](https://blog.kowatek.com/?p=6557). Rating category definitions (qualitative line between B and CCC)
- S&P: CCC is "currently vulnerable to nonpayment, and is dependent upon favorable business, financial, and economic conditions"; under adverse conditions the obligor is not likely to meet commitments. As a rule issuers facing at least a one-in-two likelihood of default are rated in the CCC category. CCC+ describes debt that looks unsustainable long term without a payment crisis within 12 months — [Maalot document citing S&P criteria](https://www.maalot.co.il/publications/mt20131212112800d.pdf) and [S&P definitions copy](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/Ausgrid%20-%201.11C-%20S%26P%20-%20Ratings%20Definitions%20-%20January%202015.pdf) (secondary copies dated 2013 to 2015, current wording not verified).
- Moody's: B is "speculative and subject to high credit risk"; Caa is "poor standing and subject to very high credit risk"; Caa1/Caa2/Caa3 map to CCC+/CCC/CCC- — [Moody's rating scales PDF](https://www.cajaruraldeasturias.com/sites/default/files/2019-08/moodys_rating_scales.pdf) and [Lithuanian Ministry of Finance mapping](https://finmin.lrv.lt/en/competence-areas/state-debt-management/credit-ratings/definitions-of-credit-ratings/).

## 2. What qualitative and hybrid frameworks (Porter, concentration, Beneish, Piotroski, governance, going concern tests) apply, and which combination does the literature find most predictive for firms with negative equity and large legacy liabilities?

### Takeaway
No source I found tests a combination specifically on negative book equity plus legacy pension liabilities. The general literature says market-and-accounting hazard models (Campbell, Hilscher, Szilagyi 2008) beat accounting-only scores in their study, Z-scores are descriptive with roughly 0.75 accuracy internationally, and each screen has a known false positive problem (Altman on intangible-heavy or negative-equity firms, Beneish flags 3.5% to 17.5% of clean firms, Piotroski is a return screen not a default model). Going concern tests (ASC 205-40, AS 2415) are a one-year liquidity test, not a leverage test.

### Cited Findings
Going concern tests
- ASC 205-40: management assesses whether conditions raise substantial doubt, defined as "probable" (ASC 450 sense, a higher bar than more-likely-than-not) that the entity cannot meet obligations as they come due within one year after the financial statements are issued; management plans count only if probable of being effectively implemented and probable of mitigating the conditions; if the plans alleviate the doubt, disclosure of principal conditions and the plans is still required (ASC 205-40-50-13) — [GAAP Dynamics reminder](https://www.gaapdynamics.com/reminders-about-going-concern-requirements-asc-205-40/), [BDO going concern](https://arch.bdo.com/Going-Concern-Assessments) (snippet level).
- AS 2415 vs ASC 205-40: Gibson Dunn notes they are closely similar in indicating circumstances and in the requirement to assess management plans but differ in the FASB definition of substantial doubt — [Gibson Dunn alert](https://gibsondunn.com/?p=58105). The AS 2415 specific procedures and evaluation window were not retrievable (gap).
- Applied example: Diebold Nixdorf's FY2022 10-K said it projected insufficient liquidity to meet obligations over the next 12 months without changes to its ABL because the borrowing base was substantially limited — [Diebold FY2022 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/28823/000002882323000078/dbd-20221231.htm) (snippet level). I found no going concern language in Unisys filings; none of the Unisys snippets mention it (absence of evidence from snippets, not verified in the full filing).

Quantitative bankruptcy prediction literature
- Bellovary, Giacomino and Akers (2007) reviewed 165 studies from 1965; multiple discriminant analysis and neural networks were most promising, shifting toward logit in the 1980s and 1990s; models with two factors were as accurate as models with 21; the recommended direction was applying existing models rather than building more — [Marquette repository record](https://epublications.marquette.edu/account_fac/25) (listed as Gissel, Giacomino, Akers there; author listing inconsistent across sources).
- Altman, Iwanicz-Drozdowska, Laitinen and Suvas (J. Int. Financial Mgmt & Accounting, 28(2), 2017): the general Z-score "works reasonably well for most countries" with accuracy of about 0.75, and has been outperformed by market-based or hazard models in some studies and performs very well in others — [vLex abstract](https://international.vlex.com/vid/financial-distress-prediction-in-855618057), [research.fi](https://research.fi/en/results/publication/0278420216). A French secondary summary says discriminant power weakens for intangible-heavy sectors (low confidence) — [eco3min.fr](https://eco3min.fr/qr/z-score-altman-detresse-entreprise/).
- Altman original: 72% accuracy two years before failure, 6% second-type error (sources disagree on whether that error is labeled false positive or false negative; check convention before comparing) — [Wikipedia Altman Z-score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altman_Z-score). Z'' variant for non-manufacturers uses book equity, zones 2.60 and 1.10 — [ABI study](https://www.abi.org/node/225662). Scholars criticize the Z-score as largely descriptive and say 80% to 90% accuracy figures overstate predictive ability (Wikipedia, same page). Accuracy rates for Z'' on non-manufacturers: not found.
- Campbell, Hilscher, Szilagyi (J. Finance 2008, 63(6), 2899-2939): a model combining accounting and market-based measures forecasts failure better than leading alternative measures; distressed stocks earned very low returns — [EconBiz record](https://econbiz.de/Record/in-search-of-distress-risk-campbell-john/10003823141) (comparison to Shumway and Altman named in the abstract was not shown in the snippet).
- Beneish (1999): the eight-variable M-score cutoff of -1.78 is the common flag; -2.22 is a more conservative cutoff; depending on cutoff the model caught about 38% to 76% of manipulators while misclassifying about 3.5% to 17.5% of non-manipulators (secondary handout; verify in the 1999 Financial Analysts Journal paper) — [Ohio State handout](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/3/40936/files/2016/12/M-Score-pa6gdy.pdf), [calculator page](https://calculator.academy/beneish-m-score-calculator/). Beneish has said he could not lift hit rates without raising false positives (same search, snippet).
- Piotroski (2000): nine signals in profitability, leverage/liquidity and operating efficiency, applied to high book-to-market firms; high F-score value stocks beat the market by 13.4% a year vs 5.9% for the whole value quintile; later work finds the effect reversed in one 12-year window after 1996 and unprofitable after trading costs in low-liquidity universes; the score is binary and designed for deep value stocks, not a default predictor — [Alpha Architect / Walkshäusl summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/?p=18498), [Walkshäusl 2020](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/44019/1/Walksh%C3%A4usl2020_Article_PiotroskiSFSCOREInternationalE.pdf), [Creighton record](https://researchworks.creighton.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Value-Stocks-and-Accounting-Screens-Has/991006117468602656).

Qualitative frameworks
- Porter-style durability, customer and contract concentration, governance: no source found that applies these to Unisys or to bankruptcy outcomes. Unisys-specific qualitative data points: Valens Research (2020) argued pensions rarely drive bankruptcy and that reliance on US government contracts made a pension-driven filing less likely (opinion, six years old, pre-deleveraging) — [Valens Research](https://www.valens-research.com/investor-essentials-daily/uis-unisys-corporation-consulting-firms-underfunded-pension-rating-agencies-panicked-wrong-reasons/) (snippet level). Brazilian tax and labor claims flagged as a pressure in a third-party Q2 2026 review — [Panabee](https://www.panabee.com/news/unisys-earnings-q2-2026-report) (low confidence).
- Moody's 2021 periodic review of DXC says its rating is constrained by "uncertain growth prospects due to its diminishing legacy IT revenue streams" — a worked example of how the agency treats business durability in this sub-sector — [AOL / Moody's notice](https://www.aol.com/news/computer-sciences-corporation-moodys-announces-220310951.html).

### Inferences
- A defensible stack for Unisys, built from the above: (1) ASC 205-40 liquidity read over 12 months (cash $324 million, undrawn ABL, no maturities to 2031 points to no substantial doubt, but I did not read the going concern paragraph); (2) pension-adjusted leverage and FCF after pension cash; (3) a hazard-style model with market data (CHS-type) because book-equity-based scores are distorted by Unisys's negative equity and accumulated pension losses. The last point is an inference; no source tested negative equity with pensions.
- False positive risk for Unisys: Z-type scores penalize negative book equity created by pension accounting (AOCI) rather than liquidity; M-score can flag large non-cash settlement charges and goodwill impairments (Q3 2026 about $150 million pension settlement, $47.2 million impairment) as accruals/earnings quality noise. Inference, not documented in a source.

### Gaps
- Primary-source figures for Beneish (1999) and Piotroski (2000) were not read; the handout numbers are secondary.
- No study located on prediction performance specifically for negative book equity plus defined benefit deficits; no head-to-head accuracy of M-score or F-score as default predictors.
- Unisys M-score, F-score and Z-score values: not computed (a script on filings data is the right method; filings were not reachable).
- Customer concentration data for Unisys (share of revenue from US federal government, top 10 clients) and governance signals: not retrieved.
- AS 2415 text and auditor procedures: not retrieved.

## 3. Which precedent companies filed, which refinanced or did pension risk transfers, and what observable numbers separated them two years before the outcome?

### Takeaway
Filers (Diebold Nixdorf 2023, Avaya 2017 and 2023, Sears 2018) had acute liquidity or refinancing stress (CCC ratings, borrowing base shortfall, multi-billion debt loads), with pensions as a secondary claim handled by the PBGC. Non-filers (Pitney Bowes, DXC, Conduent, and so far Xerox) had maturities pushed out, positive FCF or insurance annuity transfers. Unisys's own 1990s distress ended without a filing through debt reduction. Hard two-years-prior numbers were found for only some cases; the rest are gaps.
