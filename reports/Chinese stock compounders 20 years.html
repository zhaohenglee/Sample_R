Every top Chinese compounder crashed along the way

A survivor screen sets the bar at 13.9% a yearThe top 100: brands and platforms on top, shells and cycles underneathNo Chinese stock compounded at 12% without falling by halfEarnings rose up to 205 times while valuation multiples fellDurable ROE needed pricing power, a captive buyer or light assetsConclusion

The 100 best Chinese stocks over 20 years compounded at 13.9% to 27.6% a year, and every one of them fell at least 57% at some point. Ranked by total return (dividends reinvested) from 20 October 2006 to 20 October 2026, across 1,254 surviving mainland companies listed in Shanghai, Shenzhen, Hong Kong and New York, the list is led by Tencent (27.6% a year, 131x), Shanghai Fudan Microelectronics (25.8%), Grand Pharmaceutical (24.7%), Gree Electric (24.0%), Zangge Mining (22.5%) and Kweichow Moutai (21.8%), against 7.5% a year for the CSI 300 total return index and 5.4% for MSCI China in US dollars. The direct answer to the second question is no: both screens found zero Chinese stocks that compounded at 12% or more a year with a maximum drawdown shallower than 50%, and none came close to a smooth line. The closest candidates (NetEase, NARI Technology, Moutai, Gree, Tencent, Jiangsu Hengrui, Yili and, with a large caveat, China Yangtze Power) did not avoid the selloffs; they recovered faster because their earnings kept growing while the market cut their valuation multiples, and earnings growth explains a median of about 95% of their returns. Only 18 of the 100 show a sustainable return on equity (ROE), 44 sustained it partly and 38 did not; about a quarter owe much of their return to shells, reverse mergers or asset injections rather than to a durable business. Every figure rests on a survivor only universe, on price data with known vendor disagreements and on ROE from the Sina and Eastmoney aggregators rather than annual reports, so treat ranks near any cutoff as soft. This is analysis, not investment advice.

A survivor screen sets the bar at 13.9% a year

The ranking merges two price screens run on daily data to 20 October 2026. The A share screen started from the 1,201 companies on the current Shanghai and Shenzhen lists that were listed by 20 October 2006 and kept 1,085 that traded on at least 90% of window days (SSE list; SZSE list). The Hong Kong and US screen fetched all 2,771 HKEX codes and the US listed Chinese names, kept 751 continuous Hong Kong histories and classified 178 of them plus 12 US listings as mainland businesses (HKEX list; USCC list). Total return is the Yahoo adjusted close (Yahoo chart API), cross checked against an independent reconstruction from Tencent raw prices and Sina adjustment factors (Sina factors) and against stockanalysis.com (stockanalysis chart API). Where a company had both an A and an H share, the listing with the higher CAGR was kept; only Yankuang Energy is affected (H share 15.1% kept over A share 14.3%). The cutoff is a near tie: rank 100 (Guangdong Fenghua, 13.941%) beats rank 101 (China Resources Jiangzhong, 13.938%).

The bar these stocks cleared was low because the market itself compounded poorly. The CSI 300 total return index returned 4.25x (7.5% a year) and MSCI China net in US dollars 2.85x (5.4%), against 8.36x (11.2%) for the S&P 500 total return; the Hang Seng price index managed 1.4% a year (csindex CSI 300 TR; MSCI China net; Yahoo ^HSI). An investor sat through four large falls: about 72% for A shares in 2007 to 2008, 47% in 2015 to 2016, 32% in 2018 and 46% for the CSI 300 in 2021 to 2024, when MSCI China fell 64% to October 2022. The "China discount" is visible in valuations: at 30 September 2026 MSCI China traded on 10.2 times forward earnings against 17.9 for MSCI World, 43% cheaper (MSCI China factsheet; MSCI World factsheet). Citi traces part of the gap to dilution: since 2002 China's nominal GDP rose 12 times but market capitalisation rose 27 times while the indices rose only 3 times (Citi, Feb 2024).

Three caveats run through every number below. First, the universe is survivors only: companies delisted since 2006 are absent (145 Shanghai records alone carry a delisting date, and the state owned US ADRs are gone), which flatters every CAGR. Second, Yahoo mishandles some Chinese bonus issues, rights issues and 2006 to 2008 share reform resumptions; three A share errors were corrected (Henan Lingrui rose from A share rank 98 to 34, which is 43 in the combined list; Sany Heavy and Shanghai Baosight also moved up), three Hong Kong bonus issues were fixed, and vendors still disagree by more than 10% on names such as Chifeng Jilong Gold, Sichuan Road & Bridge, NARI and Yankuang. Third, several stocks traded thinly or were suspended for long stretches, which flattens their drawdowns. The tables flag each of these.

The top 100: brands and platforms on top, shells and cycles underneath

The list holds 76 A shares, 23 Hong Kong listings and one US listing (NetEase). Its median stock compounded at 16.7% a year and suffered a 77.6% maximum drawdown; 59 of the 100 hit their deepest trough in 2008, 36 fell more than 80%, and only NetEase (57.3%) and NARI (57.6%) stayed shallower than 60%. No stock had fewer than five negative calendar years out of 19. The winners cluster in consumer brands (baijiu, appliances, dairy, jewellery), Chinese medicine and pharma, internet platforms, grid equipment, autos and parts, and a long tail of commodity, chemical and materials cyclicals. ROE verdicts follow the profile notes: "yes" means ROE at or above 15% in about 8 or more of the 10 years 2016 to 2025 without a structural break; "partly" means a majority of one decade, a documented cycle or one off, or 4 to 7 of 10 years; "no" means a commodity or one off boom, thin margins, or a shell or theme driven return. The three researchers applied the rule with judgment, so borderline calls (Xinyi Glass partly, Wanhua yes, both at 8 of 10) are not perfectly uniform. ROE is weighted average ROE from Sina, with Eastmoney as fallback, for A shares, and net profit over average equity from Eastmoney for Hong Kong names (Sina financial guideline; Eastmoney annual indicators; Eastmoney HK indicators).

Columns: CAGR is the 20 year total return CAGR in listing currency; TR x is the total return multiple; Max DD is the largest peak to trough fall on daily data, shown as the size of the fall. Flags: C a price data correction was applied; S shell, reverse merger, asset injection or turnaround of a near insolvent listed company, so early ROE belongs to a different business; I illiquid or long suspensions (A shares traded on under 95% of days; Hong Kong 20% or more zero volume days; Brilliance China a 372 day suspension); P penny stock or low quality print the screen says to treat with caution; V data vendors disagree on the total return multiple by more than 5%; L first trade up to 14 market days after the window start.

No Chinese stock compounded at 12% without falling by half

The A share screen found 132 stocks at 12% or more a year and the Hong Kong and US screen 33; in neither did a single stock have a maximum drawdown shallower than 50%, none was shallower than 30%, and none combined four or fewer negative years with a straight line price path (R squared of 0.85 or more). The shallowest whole window drawdowns among the 12% compounders were Grandblue Environment (54.7%), China Yangtze Power (55.6%), Yunnan Baiyao (56.1%), NetEase (57.3%) and NARI (57.6%); an independent drawdown check on Sina adjusted prices moved no figure by 5 points or more (Yahoo chart API; Sina factors). For scale, the CSI 300 total return index fell 72.0% and had nine negative years (csindex CSI 300 TR).

Holding up in one selloff was common; holding up in all four was not. In the 2021 to 2024 discount phase PICC Property and Casualty fell only 22%, Sichuan Chuantou 28%, Zhejiang Weixing 31% and the gold miners 32% to 34%, but PICC had fallen 89% in 2008. In 2007 to 2008 Hengrui fell 39%, NetEase 42% and Tencent 47%, yet Hengrui fell 71% and Tencent 73% in 2021 to 2024. Across an 18 stock shortlist of the closest candidates, the share that fell less than their benchmark dropped from 13 of 17 in 2007 to 2008 to 5 of 17 in 2015 to 2016, 3 of 18 in 2018 and 2 of 18 in 2021 to 2024: the best businesses had a valuation cushion in the 2008 bubble, then lost it as their own multiples became the thing the market sold (Yahoo chart API; csindex CSI 300 TR).

Three things distinguished this group. They had durable ROE (Moutai never below 24.4%, Gree never below 18.9%, Tencent never below 15.1%, NetEase below 15% once, NARI never below 13.2%) with low debt or a captive customer. Their profits kept rising through the selloffs, so each fall was a multiple fall: Moutai's profit rose 34%, 1%, 30% and 60% across the four windows while its implied P/E fell 72%, 31%, 49% and 67%. And they recovered quickly: Gree regained its prior peak 12, 14, 3 and 29 months after each trough, against 79 months for the CSI 300 total return index after 2008, 53 months after 2015, and no recovery yet of its 2021 peak. Part of that speed is built in, because a list of survivors with 12% CAGR excludes the stocks that never came back. The record also decays: Hengrui was the one clear defensive name through 2016 (39% and 25% falls against the index's 72% and 46%), but its ROE dropped to 11% to 15% after 2020 under drug procurement price cuts and it fell 71% from its 2020 peak without recovering. Grandblue's shallow 2008 fall (46%) was not repeated in 2015 (55%), so it is not a stable defensive profile.

China Yangtze Power needs a specific warning. Its 55.6% maximum drawdown looks like the second shallowest in China, and in the two windows when it traded normally it was genuinely defensive (16% against the index's 30% in 2018, and 18% against 42% in 2021 to 2024). But it was suspended for restructuring from 8 May 2008 until it reopened on 18 May 2009, and from 12 June 2015 until 16 November 2015, through the worst of both crashes (cninfo, May 2008; cninfo, June 2015; cninfo, November 2015). It reopened 2% higher in 2009 while the CSI 300 had fallen 27% over the same dates and traded utility peers had fallen between 29% and 70% from that starting date at their lows. Before the 2008 freeze it had fallen 34%, in line with the index. Its true drawdown in 2008 and 2015 is unobserved, so its apparent resilience in those years came from illiquidity, not from the business.

Earnings rose up to 205 times while valuation multiples fell

For the 16 shortlisted companies with positive 2006 earnings, per share earnings growth explains a median of about 95% of the log total return, the change in P/E a small negative (median ratio 0.93, eight fell and eight rose) and dividends about 7% to 8% (Eastmoney annual indicators; Eastmoney share capital history). Tencent, Zhejiang NHU, IEIT Systems, NARI, Yili, Gree, Moutai and Grandblue all derated by 20% to 80% while earnings per share rose 14 to 205 times: Tencent's P/E fell from 28.7 to 14.1 as EPS rose 205 times in local currency, Moutai's from 30.3 to 20.3 as EPS rose 53 times. A rising multiple did visible work only for Fudan Microelectronics (P/E 8.5 to 72, on a depressed 2025 profit, about 46% of its return), China Jushi, Sanhua, Sieyuan and Yangtze Power. Inside the selloffs the pattern is starker: across 66 company windows with positive profits, profit rose in 55 and the implied P/E fell in 65.

This is the most useful finding for the "China discount" question. The winners did not escape the derating; they outgrew it. The flip side is dilution, which Citi and others blame for the market's weak per share record: Yangtze Power's profit rose 9.5 times but EPS only 4.8 times, NARI's 72 times against 33 times, Sanhua's 88 times against 22 times, because shares were issued for acquisitions (Eastmoney share capital history). The size of the market wide derating over exactly this window cannot be pinned down, because no index P/E for October 2006 was found; the CSI 300 traded on about 13 times trailing earnings in October 2026 (Danjuan CSI 300 P/E), and the Shanghai Composite was near 70 times at the 2007 peak (Siblis Research).

Durable ROE needed pricing power, a captive buyer or light assets

Sustained high ROE is rare in China: only 0.6% of A shares kept ROE above 15% in every year from 2010 to 2023, and one count found just 12 companies listed before 2013 with ten straight years above 15% to 2023 (Sina, Guosen quant team; STCN data team). The 18 "yes" names share one of four traits: brand pricing power with low capital needs (Moutai, Fenjiu, Gujing, Pien Tze Huang, Yili, Supor, Lao Feng Xiang, Gree, Haier), a platform or IP franchise (Tencent, NetEase), scale embedded in customers' products (Sanhua, Fuyao, Shenzhou, Hongfa, Essex, Wanhua's MDI oligopoly), or a captive buyer (NARI and State Grid). Payout matters too: Moutai paid 79% of 2025 profit and Supor almost all of it, which keeps the equity base small and the ROE high. Even here the moat is not permanent: Wanhua (12.4%) and Lao Feng Xiang (13.6%) dropped below 15% in 2025, and Pien Tze Huang slipped to 14.8% on raw material costs.

The 82 names without fully sustained ROE failed for five repeatable reasons. Policy price cuts ended the pharma runs: Hengrui won national procurement tenders at average generic price cuts of 74.5% (Lanjinger), and Changchun High-Tech's ROE fell from 22.6% in 2023 to 0.7% in 2025 after reimbursement listing of its long acting growth hormone; CSPC, Sino Biopharm, Huadong and Grand Pharma show the same break. Commodity and freight cycles drove rare earths, gold, coal, lithium, polysilicon and shipping, where COSCO Shipping's ROE swung from minus 42% in 2016 to 101% in 2021 (Eastmoney HK indicators, COSCO). Thin margin scale businesses such as IEIT Systems (1.5% net margin) and the cable makers grew profits from tiny bases at low ROE. Leverage carried Sichuan Road & Bridge (80% plus debt to assets) and Hisense Home Appliances. And shells and injections account for 24 names, from Zangge (a ceramics shell that bought a potash mine) to AVIC Shenyang (a pickup truck maker that received the fighter aircraft business) and Ningbo Joyson (a bankrupt shell).

The ROE evidence is thinner than the tables imply. Every figure comes from Sina or Eastmoney aggregator pages, not annual report PDFs; Sina shows no FY2016 value for any company, so that year uses Eastmoney; Eastmoney carries restated figures that can differ sharply from first published ones (Wanhua's 2017 ROE is 50.7% first published and 85.9% restated after a group asset absorption); and Hong Kong ROE uses a different definition from A share weighted ROE. Several early ROE figures belong to predecessor shell companies (Zangge, AVIC Shenyang, Hengli, Chifeng, Glarun, Hongfa, Joyson) or sit on near zero or negative equity (Grand Pharma's 308% in 2007, Hisense Home Appliances' 293% in 2010), and should not be read as the record of the business owned today. Breaks can also come from accounting: Wuliangye, which ranks just outside this list at 13.4% a year, restated its 2025 quarters and reported a 6.89% ROE after revenue fell 54.6%, partly because of a new revenue recognition basis (Wuliangye 2025 annual report, cninfo).

Conclusion

Over these 20 years the price of a 14% to 28% annual return in Chinese equities was at least one fall of 57% or more, with no exceptions in either market. The usual intuition that quality protects capital in a selloff held in 2008 and then broke down: by 2018 and 2021 to 2024 the market was selling the multiples of exactly the companies that had earned them. Durable ROE predicted how fast a stock came back, not how far it fell. For anyone using this list, that changes the question from "which Chinese stocks are safe" to "which earnings streams will keep compounding through the next derating", because a rising multiple did almost none of the work for the best names.

The list is also less of a quality roster than its ranks suggest. Only 18 names combine a top 100 return with sustained ROE; the rest owe their place to commodity spikes, policy protected boom years that have since ended, or the arithmetic of buying a near worthless shell before an asset injection. The forward risks visible in the 2025 data, namely drug procurement cuts, a baijiu demand adjustment, property linked appliance weakness and lower metal prices, all hit the same sources of ROE that carried the winners. Read as a survivor list with soft ranks near the cutoff, it is evidence about what kinds of Chinese businesses compound, not a shortlist of what to buy.
