Unisys should still be judged first on a pension adjusted liquidity and cash flow framework, but the filing grade rerun moves market signals above the agency rating map, because the market now has dated numbers and they disagree with the rating. The filings confirm the near term picture: no going concern language anywhere, an unqualified audit, liquidity of 424.0 at 30 June 2026 (cash 324.3 plus 99.7 of undrawn ABL availability), and no funded maturity before the 698.4 of 10.625% notes due 15 January 2031. They also confirm the medium term problem, and sharpen it: holding the company's own 2026 guidance flat, free cash flow after pension turns negative every year from 2027 and leaves 242.9 of cash against the 698.4 maturity, a shortfall of 455.5. That is arithmetic on stated guidance, not a forecast. The rating view (S&P B, Moody's B2, stable, last confirmed in February 2026) maps to 2.93% one year and 15.6% five year default on a secondary copy of S&P's table. The market reads worse. The notes traded at 94.5 to yield 12.29% on 7 October 2026, a spread of 7.32 points that implies a risk neutral 11.5% one year default probability at 40% recovery, 3.9 times the B rate and 37% of the way from B to CCC. The equity, at $2.22 and a market value of 161.9 against 733.5 of debt, gives a naive distance to default probability of 24.2% or 83.4% depending only on the drift assumption. The verdict therefore shifts: one year default risk stays low on cash grounds, the rating's 3% is a floor rather than a central estimate, and the market prices the 2031 refinancing closer to CCC than to B. Three gaps remain open: no filing states a mid 2026 pension deficit, neither the 2031 notes' issue rating nor Moody's own default table was retrieved, and the S&P table is a secondary copy.

Two changes need defending. Market signals move up because the earlier ranking held them back only for missing inputs, and the inputs now exist. The design case was always strong: Campbell, Hilscher and Szilagyi found a model combining accounting and market measures forecasts failure better than leading alternatives (EconBiz), and Bharath and Shumway found a naive distance to default performs as well as the full Merton model (IDEAS/RePEc). The rating map moves down because its only primary evidence is one sentence in a 10-K filed on 25 February 2026, the Q2 10-Q contains no rating sentence, and no 2026 action was found (10-K; Q2 10-Q). Since then Unisys has written off all remaining DWS goodwill and its stock has fallen from $3.66 at 30 June to $2.22. A rating that predates those events is weaker evidence than a bond print from last week. Within the market family the bond leg carries the weight, because it prices the secured claim directly; the equity leg swings from 24.2% to 83.4% on one assumption and serves as a bound, not a point estimate. Cash runway stays first because both market numbers are risk neutral or drift dependent, while the filings answer the one year question directly.

The rest of the ordering rests on the same sourced points as before. Moody's standard adjustment treats the underfunded pension as debt (IPE), which is why the agency framework still beats the accounting scores. The Altman family shows only about 0.75 accuracy internationally (vLex abstract), is blind to unfunded pensions (StockAlarm guide), and was fitted on manufacturers and industrials (MetricGate). Beneish and Piotroski screen for manipulation and value, not default (Ohio State handout; Alpha Architect). No source tested any of these models on firms combining negative book equity with a large pension deficit, so the ranking is reasoned from model design.

Cash runway clears 2026; guidance arithmetic leaves a 325 to 456 gap at 2031

On the top ranked framework Unisys is liquid for now. The debt is one instrument: 698.4 face of 10.625% senior secured notes due 15 January 2031, carrying 686.9, plus 37.3 of finance leases and 9.3 of other debt (Q2 10-Q). Scheduled maturities before 2031 are 5.4 or less a year (10-K). The 125 ABL runs to 27 June 2030, was undrawn at 30 June with 99.7 of availability, and its only financial covenant, a 1.00x fixed charge test, applies only if availability falls below 12.5 (Q2 10-Q; ABL amendment). Management says it has met all covenants and expects adequate liquidity "for at least the next twelve months", and no filing carries going concern language (Q2 10-Q). That clears the ASC 205-40 bar, which asks only whether failure within a year is probable (GAAP Dynamics). One caution: 197.6 of the 324.3 cash sits abroad, about a third of it not readily transferable.

The pension is shrinking as a claim but not yet as a cash call. The global deficit fell from 750.2 to 448.5 in 2025 (US 281.2, international 167.3), mostly because Unisys paid in 343.7, including a 250 discretionary contribution funded about 200 from the notes (10-K). Contributions run at 97 in 2026, 104 in 2027 and 241 across 2028 to 2030 (Q2 10-Q; 10-K). The springing ABL trigger is not close: the US share of 2026 is 47, and the 2027 global 104 includes international plans. The 10-K's own risk factor names the downside plainly: if operations cannot fund contributions, Unisys "may be forced to ... seek additional equity capital, waiver or bankruptcy protection".

Cash generation is the weak point. Company defined free cash flow, which already deducts pension, was −217.6 in FY2025, −74.5 in H1 2026 and +31.2 over the twelve months to June; before pension it was 127.7, −16.4 and 147.0 (unisys_metrics.md). Interest expense more than doubled to 36.8 in H1 2026 from 16.4 a year earlier. Adjusted EBITDA covers interest 3.75 times on a trailing basis, down from 9.16 in FY2024; operating cash flow covers it 1.52 times, or 3.09 before pension. Pension adjusted leverage at 30 June is 4.27x gross and 3.10x net, against the company's target of below 2.0x by end 2029 (Investor Day deck). Operations also fell short of the guide in the first half: H1 non GAAP operating margin was 5.0% against a 9% to 11% full year guide, and Q2 brought an operating loss of 32.9 after a 48.7 impairment that cleared the remaining DWS goodwill (Q2 release; Q2 10-Q).

Bond and equity markets now price Unisys between B and CCC

The market evidence the earlier report lacked now exists, and it splits by claim. The 2031 notes printed at 94.5, yield 12.29%, on 7 October 2026 through the FINRA feed, on a 200,000 face bar (TradingView FINRA). Against a 4.97% interpolated Treasury (US Treasury) that is a 7.32 point spread. The 10-Q's Level 2 fair value at 30 June implied a 94.2 price and an 8.14 point spread (Q2 10-Q), so credit spreads tightened slightly over the quarter while Treasury yields rose about 80 basis points at five years (US Treasury, June). Over the same months the stock fell from $3.66 to $2.22 on 9 October, a market value of 161.9 and −43.8% over one year (Yahoo Finance chart API). Equity is worth 0.22 times total debt. The lien structure explains the gap: the notes hold first liens on substantially all non ABL assets and the indenture bars pension obligations from ranking equal to them on collateral (Indenture), so the equity absorbs the pension and refinancing risk first.

All bond figures are risk neutral and include liquidity and risk premia, so they overstate real world default probability, and they rest on a single thin print.

The rating map supplies the physical anchor. S&P rates Unisys B and Moody's B2, both stable, as stated in the February 2026 10-K (10-K); Moody's 2025 cut cited flat revenue and debt to EBITDA near 4.5x, per a news report rather than the release (Investing.com). S&P's 1981 to 2024 averages give the B category 2.93% at one year, 10.46% at three, 15.6% at five and 22.02% at ten, and CCC/C 26.12%, 41.32%, 46.53% and 50.43%, from secondary copies of table 24 (Ledgertouch; investmentgrade.com). The bond's one year 11.5% is 3.9 times the B rate and sits 37% of the way to CCC; its cumulative 40.6% to January 2031 sits much closer to CCC's 46.5% five year rate than to B's 15.6% (unisys_metrics.json). Part of that gap is risk premium, not default risk, but not all of it. S&P reserves CCC for issuers facing at least a one in two chance of default (Maalot copy of S&P criteria), and the market is pricing Unisys in that direction while the agencies stand two notches above it.

The sensitivity runs show why these readings deserve little weight. In Z'' the retained earnings term alone contributes −4.377 at FY2025 and −5.183 at June 2026; set the accumulated deficit to zero and Z'' with its constant would be 4.639 and 4.155, comfortably safe. Moving the pension liability into equity barely moves Altman (Z'' with constant rises from 0.262 to 0.559) but cuts the Zmijewski set B probability from 97.1% to 64.9% at FY2025, so the probit leverage term is reacting to the pension as much as to debt. Grover, the lone healthy reading, flips to −0.032 once EBIT is rebuilt as pretax result plus interest, which brings the pension settlement loss and other below the line charges in. The scores measure decades of losses and charges, including the 227.7 pre tax settlement loss on the September 2025 annuity (10-K), not the ability to pay a coupon. The useful residue is narrow: positive working capital of 245.8 at June and a thin operating margin, both of which the stronger frameworks already see.

Precedents point to the 2029 to 2031 refinancing

The precedent companies show what usually triggers a filing: debt service and refinancing against falling earnings, with pensions as a secondary claim. Diebold Nixdorf was cut to the triple C category about 13 months before its June 2023 prepackaged Chapter 11, and its FY2022 10-K warned of insufficient liquidity because its ABL borrowing base was constrained (Creditflux; Diebold 10-K). Avaya filed twice and the PBGC took its salaried plan (ChannelWeb); Sears's pension became an unsecured claim of about $800 million (AI-CIO). Non filers pushed maturities out and kept cash flow positive: Pitney Bowes generated $358 million of free cash flow in 2025 (Business Wire), while Xerox, with 2028 maturities and leverage near 6x, was cut to Caa2 in March 2026 (Action Intell). Unisys itself escaped an expected filing around 1990 by cutting debt from $4 billion to $1.1 billion by 1993 (Washington Technology).

Today Unisys shows none of Diebold's warning signs: no CCC rating, no liquidity warning, an undrawn ABL with ample availability. It lacks Pitney Bowes's main defence, free cash flow after all claims, and its bond trades well below what its rating implies. The indenture frames the refinancing. The notes become callable at 105.313 in January 2028, falling to par in January 2030, and new pari passu debt beyond a fixed basket requires total net leverage of 1.70x or less (Indenture). The ABL matures in June 2030, about seven months before the notes, so both facilities need replacing within the same window. A clean refinancing therefore needs either the company's sub 2.0x pension inclusive leverage target by 2029 or a lender willing to accept something close to today's 12% yield.

Conclusion

The filings did not overturn the earlier verdict so much as remove its comfort. The snippet round leaned on a near par bond and a stable B rating to put risk near 3% a year; the filed fair values and the FINRA price history show the notes already fell to the low to mid 80s in March, and the dated October print prices a cumulative risk to maturity that looks more like CCC than B. The best reading is now a range with a clear shape: low one year risk, because liquidity and the maturity wall are both confirmed, and a five year risk that runs well above the 15.6% B average, because the runway arithmetic shows no path to repaying the 2031 notes from cash and the market knows it. The rating is the lagging indicator here; S&P reports that 91.7% of 2024 defaulters rated at the start of the year had been cut to CCC+ or lower before they defaulted (S&P), so a stable B two notches above that line is the start of the warning sequence, not its absence.

The next three months will test this. The Q3 10-Q in early November should confirm or deny the September annuity, report the settlement charge and possibly a funded status that fills the mid 2026 deficit gap. The test that matters most is whether second half free cash flow reaches +49.5, the arithmetic remainder of the full year guide, after a first half margin of 5%. A miss there, a downgrade toward CCC or Caa, or the bond falling back toward its March lows would mark the shift from refinancing risk to default risk. A hit, with the bond holding in the mid 90s, would leave the 2028 call date as the first real window to refinance on survivable terms.
